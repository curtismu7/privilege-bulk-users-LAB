Generated by `npm run talk-track`. Do not edit by hand.

Before you start, say which mode you are in: the header toggle, the `Mode:` chip and the MOCK or REAL modal that opens the page all show it. **Mock** is simulated (an in-process stand-in for PingOne and the Privilege console on this machine, with made-up people on `.example` addresses; an illustrative sample, not a recording). **Real** is owner-run, refused until the owner opts in, and creates people and group memberships in real environments.

Press P (or the Present button) for presenter mode: larger text, and the `Mode:` chip stays on screen. Esc leaves it. Reset (header) puts the demo back to its start: the job and its plan, the audit history and the Mock people are cleared, the Real writes switch goes off, and the diagram boxes return to their places.

## 1. Open on the problem

**Do:** stay on the Guide. Point at the title and the first section, "One click per person does not scale".

**Say:** "Privilege only knows the people who live in its own PingOne environment and are members of one group. A large company does not click five thousand times, and afterwards nobody can say who was done and who was skipped. What they need is a run that is repeatable, idempotent, reportable and rate-limit aware. That is what this lab shows, on made-up people."

**Why it matters:** it names the four tests the rest of the demo passes, so the audience watches for them.

## 2. Walk the Guide

**Do:** scroll through "How Privilege knows a user", then the two-routes table, then Route A step by step (token, group, source, snapshot, create, add, verify), then "What we have not verified".

**Say:** "Route A is the one we have run: a script that is in daily use. Route B is the Privilege console's own API; it is in the swagger, but it is UNVERIFIED against a real console. Every command on this page is curl with `$PLACEHOLDER` variables: there is no real id and no token on it. Notice step 4: we read the destination once and decide in memory, instead of one lookup per person. And here is what we have not verified: how many calls PingOne allows, how a person created this way signs in, whether the console's list pages. We say so rather than guess."

**Why it matters:** the page teaches the mechanism first, and it is honest about its limits: every claim has a source or is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 3. The trap: Missing role

**Do:** open Run. Choose route A, the **Missing role** scenario and 100 people, then press **Plan (dry run)**. Point at the blocking `destination_empty` warning. Type `REGISTER` and the number: Apply stays disabled. Do not tick "I understand".

**Say:** "The worker app here lacks the Identity Data Admin role in Privilege's environment. PingOne does not answer 403. It answers 200 and an empty list, which looks exactly like an empty environment. A naive script concludes that nobody is registered and tries to create everyone, and PingOne refuses the half who already exist. The plan turns that empty list into a blocking warning. Apply stays off until a person has read it and ticked the box, and even then it needs the count typed back."

**Why it matters:** the worst failures of a bulk job are silent. This one is caught before anything is written. (Optional, Mock only: tick the box and apply, and the run stops itself after the first failures with `circuit_open`: one cause, not that many bad people.)

## 4. Run the Realistic scenario

**Do:** choose the **Realistic** scenario and 500 people, leave concurrency at its default and press **Plan (dry run)**. Read the counts: create and add, add to the group, in sync, conflict, invalid. Open the results table, filter on conflict and open one row. Then type `REGISTER` and the "to apply" number and press **Apply**. While it runs, point at the call numbers under the progress bar: calls, retries, **429 answers (rate limit)**, **Waited (back-off)**, calls per second.

**Say:** "The plan wrote nothing; it only decided. A handful of people have a username that belongs to someone else, so they are conflicts: the lab will not add them, a person decides. A few have no email, so they are invalid and never sent. Now apply, with the count typed back. Watch the 429 counter: the service says slow down, and one 429 pauses every worker for the Retry-After, not only the one that was told off, and then the run carries on. A person created a moment ago can answer 404 on the group add until PingOne catches up, so we wait and retry. Nobody failed because of either. In Mock the waits are shortened, so a one-second Retry-After becomes a few milliseconds: the counter is real, the seconds are not. How many calls PingOne really allows is UNVERIFIED: this lab learns it from the 429s."

**Why it matters:** a bulk job that hammers the service is a job that gets throttled or blocked. This one listens, waits together and finishes, and every person ends with a row you can download as JSON or CSV.

## 5. Plan again finds nothing

**Do:** press **Verify (plan again)**.

**Say:** "Same source, same decision procedure, and now: zero to apply. Everyone who was created is in sync. The only rows left are the conflicts and the invalid ones, and those need a human. Run the whole job twice and the second run changes nothing: that is what idempotent means, and it is how you prove a run, not by trusting a log. The count typed back is the gate, the report is the receipt, and planning again is the proof."

**Why it matters:** it closes the loop on repeatable and idempotent, and gives the customer a check they can run themselves after any real run.

## Caveats to say out loud

- Mock is a mock: the people, the numbers and the failures are made up and deterministic; it is an illustrative sample, not a recording.
- Route A follows a script the owner runs; **nothing in Real was run while this lab was built**. The first Real run is the owner's, and `docs/live-checks.md` is the checklist for it.
- Route B is UNVERIFIED against any real console: it has only ever run against the Mock.
- This lab never deletes. People it creates in Real are removed by hand in PingOne.
- Anything on the Guide's "What we have not verified" list is UNVERIFIED here: rate limits, how a new person signs in, the onboarding-link step, whether the console's list pages.
